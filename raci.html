Why this owner, and what makes it real

1. Budget · Chief Financial Officer

The CFO controls firmwide investment, so only this role can weigh rival claims on one pool of money. That matters most at G1 and G2, when business units push for near-term returns.

Enforced by: funds for each phase are released only after the gate review.

2. Architecture · Chief Information Officer

The CIO runs the shared platform that C2 Cloud & Platform Modernisation builds, so any exception affects every business line. One final say lets W2 and W3 teams be refused without a committee dispute.

Enforced by: an architecture review before any W2 or W3 build starts.

3. Data · Chief Data & Analytics Officer

C1 Enterprise Data Governance creates the rules W1 and W3 depend on, so one executive must own them across business lines. The roadmap cites a data mesh of owned data products, which fits a single enterprise owner.

Enforced by: data products are opened only through the owner approval process.

4. Priorities · Chief Operating Officer

W1, W2 and W3 share the same platforms and teams, so someone above any single business line must rank them. This stops one unit from putting its own pilot first.

Enforced by: the ranked list is set at G2, and changes need COO sign-off.

5. Vendors · Chief Procurement Officer

Cloud and AI providers bring long-term cost, security and legal exposure, so one role should own the contract terms. Keeping this apart from the CIO stops the platform owner approving its own suppliers.

Enforced by: no C2 or W3 provider contract is signed without procurement approval.

6. AI approval · Chief Risk Officer

R1 Hyperautomation & AI Decision Support scales AI into operations, and the roadmap requires model-risk approval before go-live. The CRO is independent of the teams that gain from launching, so the sign-off is credible.

Enforced by: release is blocked until the model-risk sign-off is recorded.

7. Journeys · CEO, Consumer & Community Banking

R2 Personalised Digital Experiences scales W1 Omnichannel Customer Experience across segments, and the roadmap calls for a journey owner per segment. The CCB CEO owns the consumer business whose roadmaps clash.

Enforced by: each journey has a named owner, and cross-channel changes go through that owner.
